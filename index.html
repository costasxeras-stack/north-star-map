One number to steer by, and the fifteen under it.

Duolingo steers by daily active learners. This map breaks that North Star into acquisition, activation, engagement, retention and the monetisation that pays for it, with every public number linked to its source. Tap any metric for its definition, latest value and the levers that move it.

North Star: Daily active users (DAUs)

Learners who use the app or the learning part of the website on a given day, averaged over the quarter. Daily use is the habit that makes learning work, and Duolingo's own growth model is built to grow it.

Reading the treeUsers are growing much faster than payers. DAUs rose 29% while paid subscribers rose 14%, so paid subscribers per DAU fell from 22.8% to 20.2%, and bookings per DAU from $5.58 to $4.87 a quarter (both derived). Duolingo says this is deliberate: in its Q4 2025 letter it estimated more than $50M of bookings given up in 2026 to remove friction from the free experience and grow users. The tree needs a counter-metric next to the North Star, which is why monetisation sits beside it, not under it.

Why retention of current users is the biggest lever

Duolingo's growth model splits every user into one of seven states each day. DAUs are the sum of the four active ones. Moving users between states is what every metric in the tree does.

NewFirst day ever
CurrentActive today and in the past week
ReactivatedActive today, last seen 8 to 30 days ago
ResurrectedActive today, last seen over 30 days ago
At-risk weeklyActive in the past week, not today
At-risk monthlyActive in the past month, not the past week
DormantInactive for 30 days or more

Duolingo simulated growing each transition rate by 2% month over month and found the current user retention rate (CURR) moved DAUs the most. The simulator below rebuilds that model. Try it.

If I owned this tree

  1. Keep DAUs as the North Star, and pair it with paid subscribers per DAU as the counter-metric. A user-first year is a choice. The pair makes the cost visible every week instead of once a quarter.
  2. Report retention by user state internally, the way the growth model does. CURR, new user retention and resurrection tell three different stories that one DAU number hides.
  3. Give acquisition a quality metric, not only a volume one. Partnerships inside games brought "millions of new and returning users" in Q3 2026. The question is how many became current users 30 days later.
  4. Treat DAU/MAU as the engagement health check. It rose from 37.3% to 42.4% (derived): more of the monthly base now shows up daily.
